# Plain summary

This paper sets out a speculative picture of the multiverse, called the Mandarano Model and nicknamed "the cable," and lists what it predicts. In the model, every history of our universe is a string held under tension by a stationary present moment, the "blade." Histories that split recently stay in step and bundle into strands of nineteen strings; seven strands make a rope of 133 strings; all the ropes allowed by our laws of physics make the cable. Most of the dark sector is read as an effect of the neighbors: dark matter is the gravitational "shadow" of the six strings touching ours, spread into halos as it crosses the gap between strings, and dark energy is the tension carried by the outer ring of twelve. The strand is slowly unwinding, which the model reads as the arrow of time and as weakening dark energy. From this picture come concrete expectations about dark energy, dark matter detection, the fixed dark-to-ordinary matter ratio, offsets in colliding galaxy clusters, galaxy rotation curves, and the Hubble tension. Each is stated below with what would count against it.

# Honesty statement

Read this first.

1. **Who wrote this.** The author is a screenwriter, not a physicist. The model began as a way to make sense of time travel stories and grew into a picture of the multiverse that touches real cosmology. It accompanies a long-form video documentary.
2. **What it is.** This is a speculative *model*, not a theory. It has not been peer reviewed. Nothing in it is proven, and nothing in it claims that established physics is wrong.
3. **What version 1.0.1 contains.** No computation. There are no fits, no likelihoods, no simulations, and no numerical test results in this version. Where numbers appear, they are either published measurements (with citations) or simple arithmetic on the model's counting rules, labeled as numerology or ansatz. Every prediction's status is "untested in v1."
4. **What it is for.** It is written to be broken. The parameters are named, the datasets are listed, and the pass and fail criteria are set in advance (see MULTIVERSE_TESTS_v1.md) so that anyone can check the model against data and report what they find, including failures.
5. **Numerology stays numerology.** The headline match between 1:6:12 and the cosmic energy budget is a coincidence until someone shows otherwise. It is presented because it motivated the model, not as evidence for it.

# 1. The model in brief

All of Section 1 is **Tier 3** except where a Tier 1 or Tier 2 anchor is named.

## 1.1 The everpresent now (the blade)

The model posits a single, physically real present moment, called the everpresent now or the blade. It cuts across every history at once.

- **The blade is stationary; the histories move.** Each history is pictured as a slowly turning coil, and every moment rides it up through a blade that never moves, the way water climbs an Archimedes screw that only rotates. Whether a knife descends through a stack of slices or the stack rises through a fixed knife, the slices are identical, so the choice costs nothing physically.
- **Its clock is cosmic time.** The blade is identified with the cosmic time of standard cosmology, the proper time of observers at rest relative to the cosmic microwave background (Tier 1 as a bookkeeping coordinate). The model promotes this foliation from bookkeeping to reality.
- **It is built to agree with relativity.** The model's stated rule is that no measurement changes: it is meant to reproduce every tested prediction of special and general relativity. The precedent is the Lorentz and Poincaré formulation of relativity with an undetectable preferred frame, which makes the same predictions as Einstein's (Tier 1 history; the choice between them was settled by simplicity, not experiment).
- **The now is thick.** It has a leading edge (the bow), a crest, and a wake. Ahead of the crest is a region of possibility; behind it, settled history. Motion relative to the blade and depth in a gravitational potential press an observer forward into the leading edge, which the model uses as a picture of time dilation. The leading edge itself is the speed of light: light rides it and nothing with mass reaches it.
- **Quantum possibilities live ahead of the blade.** Neighboring histories share possibilities there (the model's reading of interference) and settle on outcomes as moments cross the crest. In the model's version of the transactional handshake, confirmation waves may run backward in time only as far as the now, never into settled history.

## 1.2 Strings under tension

Lay a history on its side and it is a timeline. The model draws each history as a string running from the Big Bang through the present into the future, held taut by the now. Like a chain draped over a peg, tension is greatest at the now and falls off toward the deep past and the far future.

## 1.3 Plucks and the backward ripple

Time travel to the past, in the model, means stepping off the blade and acting somewhere along one's own string. An action there is a **pluck**, which does two things at once: the whole string swings sideways into a slightly different shape, and ripples run away from the spot in both directions.

- **The forward ripple** travels toward the present and carries the change.
- **The backward ripple** runs into the looser string of the deeper past. On a looser string a wave slows, and the model's string is damped, so the backward ripple dies almost at once, smoothing the moments just behind the change so that the history leading up to it stays consistent.
- **Plucks never create new strings.** They reshape the string one is on, and synced neighbors swing with it. Branching happens on its own, at the now, as histories fall out of step; time travel bends rather than branches.
- **Diluted deep plucks.** A pluck in the loose deep past weakens as it climbs into tighter string; small plucks fade completely ("history heals").

## 1.4 Stress and strain: elastic, yield, snap

The model borrows the engineering stress-strain curve (Tier 1 as materials science; its use for time is Tier 3).

- **Elastic zone:** small plucks spring back fully.
- **Yield point:** beyond it, the change is permanent. The model calls the yield point a timeline's escape velocity.
- **Springback:** past yield, part of the old shape still returns.
- **Snap:** pluck hard enough and a sliver of string breaks away, goes slack, and falls away. Breaks happen near the now, where tension is highest; the main string recoils.
- **Counter-plucks** can approach a previous state but never restore it exactly.

## 1.5 Strands, ropes and the cable

- **Sync versus divergence.** Neighboring histories pull each other into step through the shared now, as pendulum clocks on a common beam synchronize (Huygens' observation of 1665; modern analysis in Bennett et al. 2002, Tier 1). Decoherence pushes them apart. The model treats the multiverse as the balance between the two.
- **A strand** is a family of histories synced tightly enough to behave almost as one; its edge is the coherence horizon. Packing equal circles gives exactly six around one, then twelve around those: $N_s = 1 + 6 + 12 = 19$ strings per strand (Tier 1 geometry; also the classic 1-6-12 layout of 6x19 wire rope strand). The model's string sits at the center, the six are its touching neighbors, and the twelve form the outer ring.
- **A rope** is one strand surrounded by six more: $N_r = 7 \times 19 = 133$ strings (the 7x19 construction of aircraft control cable is the engineering receipt).
- **The cable** is every rope our laws of physics allow, wound together.
- **Distance is a family tree.** Histories that split recently share a strand; centuries ago, another strand of the same rope; billions of years ago, another rope.
- **Inside a rope, neighbors still feel each other** faintly through the now. This is the model's one deliberate departure from standard quantum mechanics, in which decohered branches effectively never interact again. Polchinski (1991) showed that certain nonlinear modifications of quantum mechanics, constrained to forbid faster-than-light signaling, allow communication between branches (Tier 2 anchor).

## 1.6 Voids at every level, and the Void

Every level has gaps: small ones between strings, larger ones between strands, and the largest, the **Void** (capital V), between ropes. Small gaps can be crossed with an improbable shove or at weak points where tension pulls strands close enough to touch. Across the Void between ropes, the model agrees with standard quantum mechanics: nothing crosses, at least not often and not on purpose. The model tentatively reads black holes as one-way exits from a rope into the Void.

## 1.7 Many-worlds branches as fuzz

Everett's relative-state formulation (Everett 1957; Tier 2 as an interpretation) implies vast numbers of branches. The model reads nearly all of them as **fuzz**: frayed fibers too small and too light to hold their shape against the tension of the main strings. Only a difference large enough to count as a pluck becomes a string of its own, so the 19 and 133 counts are the *primary* differences of local reality, and each string is a bundle of near-identical branches. The Tier 2 anchor is Wallace (2012), who argues that the number of Everettian branches is not well defined and depends on coarse-graining. The model picks a coarse-graining. It also bets that the multiverse is huge but not infinite.

## 1.8 Locality

Differences between histories spread no faster than light. A history that split from ours a time $t_{\rm split}$ ago differs from ours only inside a region of radius

$$
r_{\rm diff} = c\, t_{\rm split}.
$$

A split last Tuesday differs within a few light-days; a split a century ago, within a hundred light-years. The Tier 2 anchor is Deutsch and Hayden (2000), who give a local account of information flow in many-worlds. Strand membership is therefore local in space as well as time: every place has its own six touching neighbors.

## 1.9 The dimension ladder

The model repeats one unit, a point plus three directions, three times, for eleven dimensions in all (the count matches M-theory's by coincidence only; the two have nothing to do with each other).

| Dimensions | Name | Model meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 0D to 3D | Space | A point plus length, width and height. The 0D point has no size and is not counted as a dimension. |
| 4D | Timespace | The whole history of a universe folded into a point. |
| 5D, 6D | Timespace | Sideways directions between neighboring histories. |
| 7D | Timespace | Along one's own history; riding it at one second per second is being alive. |
| 8D | Cablespace | The whole cable (every history our laws allow) folded into a point. |
| 9D to 11D | Cablespace | Directions between cables: other universes with different laws or constants. |

Everything from 0D to 7D is our universe under our laws. Universes with different laws are other cables, not branches of ours. Tegmark's Level II multiverse of bubble universes with different effective constants (Tegmark 2003; Tier 2) is the closest published relative.

# 2. The dark sector mapping

This section is **Tier 3** throughout, with Tier 1 measurements and Tier 2 anchors named. The parameter names used here are shared with the companion equation sheet: $N_s$, $N_r$, $f_{\rm leak}$, $\epsilon_{\rm far}$, $w_0$, $w_a$, $\tau_{\rm lag}$, $\sigma_{\rm ref}$.

## 2.1 The 1:6:12 numerology (labeled numerology)

By count, a strand of $N_s = 19$ gives

$$
\frac{1}{19} \approx 5.3\%, \qquad \frac{6}{19} \approx 31.6\%, \qquad \frac{12}{19} \approx 63.2\%.
$$

The Planck 2018 base $\Lambda$CDM parameters (Planck Collaboration 2020; Tier 1) give approximately $\Omega_b \approx 4.9\%$, a cold dark matter share of about $26.4\%$ (about $26.6\%$ if computed as $\Omega_m - \Omega_b$, which includes the small massive-neutrino contribution), and $\Omega_\Lambda \approx 68.5\%$. The geometry fixed the number 19 before any comparison was made, which is the only thing that makes the match interesting. **It is numerology.** It is not evidence, and the model does not rest on it.

## 2.2 Dark matter as the shadow of the six touching strings

The six touching strings split from ours most recently, so by locality their matter sits almost exactly where ours does. In the model, gravity is shared mainly where strings touch: the neighbors' ordinary matter pulls on ours. That pull is dark matter: gravitating, never colliding with anything here, and never detectable as a particle, because its source is next door. The Tier 2 relatives are mirror matter and hidden-brane matter, and the idea that gravity alone can leave a brane.

A fixed number of companion strings gives a fixed ratio of dark to ordinary matter. Both dilute as $a^{-3}$ in the expansion, so the ratio stays constant over cosmic time. This is consistent with standard cosmology (Tier 1), so it is a consistency check rather than a discriminating test, but it is one the model passes by construction.

## 2.3 Refraction halos

Because the touching neighbors' galaxies are, by locality, nearly identical to ours, "blurred copies" cannot make halos. Instead, crossing the gap between strings spreads the pull, as light behind frosted glass arrives as a soft glow. The extra gravity lands as a broad halo around each galaxy. As an ansatz, the shadow density is the neighbors' baryon distribution convolved with a spreading kernel $K$ of width $\sigma_{\rm ref}$:

$$
\rho_{\rm sh}(\mathbf{x}, t) \;=\; (1 - f_{\rm leak}) \sum_{i=1}^{6} \int K_{\sigma_{\rm ref}}(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y})\, \tilde{\rho}_{b,i}(\mathbf{y}, t)\, d^3 y ,
$$

where $\tilde{\rho}_{b,i}$ is neighbor $i$'s baryon density evaluated with the lag of Section 2.6. The kernel's shape and whether $\sigma_{\rm ref}$ is a universal constant are open. Because the convolution moves mass around without changing its total, the global ratio stays fixed while the local ratio can vary from galaxy to galaxy.

## 2.4 The leak, taken up by the outer twelve as tension

The count overshoots the dark matter share (31.6% against about 26.5%) and undershoots dark energy (63.2% against 68.5%). The model's proposal: a fraction $f_{\rm leak}$ of the six neighbors' pull is lost in transit into the voids between strings and arrives as stretch instead of weight. The outer twelve take it up, and as the outer ring tightens around the strand, the lost pull is stored as tension. What dark matter loses, dark energy gains. In shares,

$$
s_b = \frac{1}{19}, \qquad s_{\rm dm} = \frac{6\,(1 - f_{\rm leak})}{19}, \qquad s_{\rm de} = \frac{12 + 6 f_{\rm leak}}{19}.
$$

**Arithmetic only (ansatz, not a fit):**

| $f_{\rm leak}$ | $s_b$ | $s_{\rm dm}$ | $s_{\rm de}$ | Dark-to-ordinary, $6(1-f_{\rm leak})$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (count only) | 5.3% | 31.6% | 63.2% | 6.0 |
| 1/6 (about 0.167) | 5.3% | 26.3% | 68.4% | 5.0 |
| Planck 2018 (measured) | 4.9% | 26.4% to 26.6% | 68.5% | about 5.36 |

**One pre-registered role for the leak: fixed by the expansion history, tested on the ratio.** "About one sixth" is the *expected* value, the narration's round number; it is not a fitted one. The value the model is held to is fitted from the expansion history: DESI DR2 BAO, the CMB and supernovae (Pantheon+ and DES SN in separate runs, never combined), which together measure the total matter density $\Omega_m$. In the model the matter share is $s_b + s_{\rm dm} = (7 - 6 f_{\rm leak})/19$, so the fitted $\Omega_m$ gives

$$
f_{\rm leak} = 1 - \frac{19\,\Omega_m - 1}{6}.
$$

At exactly one sixth, $\Omega_m = 6/19 \approx 0.316$. To keep the fix apart from the test, the fix is registered in advance to use the CMB only through its compressed distance priors, the shift parameter $R$ and the acoustic scale $\ell_A$ (Chen, Huang & Wang 2019), with the baryon density taken from big-bang nucleosynthesis (the deuterium determination of Cooke, Pettini & Steidel 2018), not from Planck's $\omega_b$. Planck's $\omega_b$ and $\omega_c$ never enter the fix.

With $f_{\rm leak}$ fixed, the dark-to-ordinary ratio becomes a prediction: $R_{\rm pred} = 6\,(1 - f_{\rm leak})$, which is 5.0 at exactly one sixth. It passes or fails against Planck 2018 (TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing): $\omega_c/\omega_b = 0.1200/0.02237 = 5.36 \pm 0.065$ (uncertainties propagated as uncorrelated). The tolerance is set in advance in MULTIVERSE_TESTS_v1.md (test T2). Stated now, honestly: at one sixth the prediction sits about 5.6 $\sigma$ below Planck, counting Planck's uncertainty alone, so the test is expected to fail unless the expansion fit lands well below one sixth (Open Problem 10).

**Independence caveat.** $R$ and $\ell_A$ come from the same Planck maps as the $\omega_c/\omega_b$ the fix is tested against, and $R$ carries the CMB's measure of the matter density. Leaving Planck's $\omega_b$ and $\omega_c$ out of the fix reduces the overlap between fix and test; it does not remove it.

Three honest remarks:

1. **One fitted number fixes both gaps by construction,** because the shares must sum to 100%. The match in the dark matter and dark energy columns is therefore not independent evidence.
2. **What information remains:** the ordinary matter share (5.3% against 4.9%) is untouched by the leak, and the ratio, now a registered test. At one sixth the leak overcorrects it (about 5 to 1 against the measured 5.36). Whether one value of the leak can serve both the shares and the ratio is listed as Open Problem 10.
3. **Share, not rising density.** The leaked pull must enter as a share of the budget, not as a dark energy density that grows over time, or it would fight the weakening bet of Section 2.7. The companion equation sheet maps the leak onto published interacting dark energy models (Tier 2; for example the review by Wang et al. 2016) purely as a starting point; whether the leak is an energy transfer at all is open.

The Tier 2 anchor for gravity "leaking" and mimicking acceleration is the Dvali, Gabadadze and Porrati brane model (Dvali, Gabadadze & Porrati 2000), whose original self-accelerating branch was later disfavored by data and stability problems.

## 2.5 The far-string pull

Every other string in the strand and every strand in the rope adds a pull of its own, fainter with distance. Individually negligible, collectively not zero. Farther strings mean more crossings and more leak, so their pull is expected to arrive mostly as smooth tension rather than clumped weight. The model names this contribution $\epsilon_{\rm far}$ and expects it to be small, $\epsilon_{\rm far} \ll 1$ as a fraction of the dark sector, and smoother than halo dark matter.

## 2.6 The lag, with carried motion

The hardest exam is the Bullet Cluster (1E 0657-558). Its hot gas, most of the ordinary matter, was slowed in the collision, while most of the mass, mapped by weak lensing, stayed with the galaxies (Clowe et al. 2006; Tier 1). The time since core passage is about 0.1 to 0.2 Gyr (Markevitch et al. 2002). The naive shadow of the neighbors' gas should follow their gas. It does not.

The model's answer: the crossing between strings is not instantaneous. The pull arriving now left the neighbors a time $\tau_{\rm lag}$ ago. And it carries its source's motion. The Tier 1 anchor is Carlip (2000): in general relativity, the aberration expected from a finite speed of gravity is almost exactly canceled by velocity-dependent terms, so the field of a uniformly moving source points at its current, extrapolated position rather than its retarded one. By analogy, the model's shadow points to where its source would be had it kept moving:

$$
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}(t) \;=\; \mathbf{x}(t - \tau_{\rm lag}) + \mathbf{v}(t - \tau_{\rm lag})\,\tau_{\rm lag}.
$$

The neighbors' gas stopped; its shadow, sent before the crash, "has not gotten the news" and keeps going with the galaxies. A pure lag without carried motion fails: it puts the shadow on the wrong side. For the rescue to work, $\tau_{\rm lag}$ must exceed the time since the Bullet Cluster's core passage, about 150 Myr. **Nothing in the model yet derives $\tau_{\rm lag}$.** The Bullet Cluster may have scraped a pass; it has not been passed.

## 2.7 The twist, unwinding, and DESI

In real rope, twist and tension are coupled: twisting tightens a strand and pulling a laid rope makes it try to untwist (Tier 1 rope mechanics). In the model, twist is the master setting. Tight twist presses strings together (strong sync); loose twist lets them drift (divergence wins). As the twist releases, edge strings drift out of step, so strand membership changes over cosmic time.

Dark energy is read as the strand's lengthwise stretch, living in the outer twelve because they wrap the longest path. A strand that is slowly unwinding loses tension, so **dark energy should be weakening**. In the Chevallier-Polarski-Linder (CPL) parameterization (Chevallier & Polarski 2001; Linder 2003),

$$
w(a) = w_0 + w_a\,(1 - a),
$$

the model expects $w_0 > -1$ today and a dark energy density that is falling now. DESI's second data release, combined with the CMB, prefers $w_0 > -1$, $w_a < 0$ over a cosmological constant at 3.1 $\sigma$, and at 2.8 to 4.2 $\sigma$ when supernovae are added, depending on the sample (DESI Collaboration 2025b). Results in 2026 moved the other way: the DESI DR2 Lyman-alpha Alcock-Paczyński analysis reports 2.7 $\sigma$ for DESI plus CMB and 3.2 $\sigma$ with supernovae (DESI Collaboration 2026); the recalibrated DES supernova sample (DES-Dovekie) lowers the DES-based preference from 4.2 to 3.2 $\sigma$ (Popovic et al. 2026); and the DES Year 6 combined analysis finds $w = -0.981^{+0.021}_{-0.022}$ in $w$CDM (DES Collaboration 2026). None of these reaches 5 $\sigma$. The model sticks with weakening. If the data settle on a constant, it loses.

The Tier 2 anchor for a decaying dark energy that is the energy of a spring-like force between neighboring branes is the Steinhardt-Turok cyclic model (Steinhardt & Turok 2002). The model's version: a fully unwound strand is wound back up for the next turn of the spiral; the Penrose conformal cyclic cosmology (Penrose 2010; Tier 2) is the other route the model keeps open.

## 2.8 The arrow of time: from wound to unwound

Why did the universe begin in such an extraordinarily low-entropy state? Penrose (1989) put the odds of a beginning that ordered at one part in $10^{10^{123}}$ (Tier 2 estimate; the puzzle itself is widely recognized). The model's picture: the beginning was maximum twist, every string pressed tight against its neighbors and every bit of tension stored. Ever since, the strand has been unwinding: strings drift, histories diverge, entropy rises. The arrow of time points from wound to unwound, and the pace of the now is the pace of the unwinding. **This is a picture, not a solution.** It moves Penrose's question (who wound it?) rather than answering it.

# 3. Predictions

Each prediction lists the claim, what the model expects, what would falsify it, the relevant data (details in MULTIVERSE_DATASETS_v1.md), and its status. The pre-registered pass and fail criteria are in MULTIVERSE_TESTS_v1.md. **Every status in version 1.0.1 is "untested in v1."**

## P1. Dark energy is weakening

- **Claim (Tier 3):** dark energy is the tension of an unwinding strand, so it is decreasing with time.
- **The model expects:** $w_0 > -1$ and $w_a < 0$ in the CPL parameterization, with the preference for this quadrant over $\Lambda$CDM growing, not shrinking, as DESI and successor surveys add data.
- **Would falsify:** future combined BAO, CMB and supernova data that are consistent with $w_0 = -1$, $w_a = 0$ while excluding the model's quadrant at high significance; or a robust detection of dark energy *strengthening* today ($w_0 < -1$).
- **Open sub-question:** CPL best fits from DESI imply that $w(a)$ was below $-1$ in the past and crossed $-1$ at intermediate redshift. A strand that has unwound monotonically since the beginning would most naturally give $w \geq -1$ at all times. Whether the model allows a past phantom regime is not settled; the equation work must decide before the test is run.
- **Relevant data:** DESI DR2 BAO, DESI DR2 Lyman-alpha, Planck 2018, Pantheon+, DES SN 5-year (and DES-Dovekie).
- **Status:** untested in v1.

## P2. No direct detection of a dark matter particle

- **Claim (Tier 3):** dark matter is gravity from neighboring strings; there is no dark matter particle here to detect.
- **The model expects:** direct-detection experiments keep reporting results consistent with background, apart from statistical fluctuations. The LZ single nuclear-recoil event announced on 1 September 2026, at about 2.6 $\sigma$ global significance (a roughly 0.5% chance from known backgrounds), is expected to remain a fluctuation as more exposure accumulates.
- **Would falsify:** a confirmed, reproducible non-gravitational detection (5 $\sigma$ or better, ideally in more than one experiment or target) of a particle whose properties can account for the cosmological dark matter. The model's author has stated plainly that this would put the idea "in serious trouble."
- **Relevant data:** LZ results (LZ Collaboration 2025; the 2026 announcement), and other direct-detection experiments.
- **Status:** untested in v1.

## P3. The dark-to-ordinary ratio is fixed over cosmic time

- **Claim (Tier 3):** a fixed number of neighbors with a fixed leak gives a fixed ratio $\Omega_{\rm dm}/\Omega_b = 6(1 - f_{\rm leak})$ at all epochs.
- **The model expects:** no evolution of the dark-to-baryon ratio with redshift, averaged over large volumes.
- **Would falsify:** a robust detection that the cosmic dark-to-baryon ratio differs between epochs (for example between the CMB era and late-time large-scale structure) beyond what standard physics explains.
- **Caution:** $\Lambda$CDM also predicts a constant ratio, so this is a consistency test rather than a discriminating one. It becomes discriminating if the leak is modeled as a continuous energy transfer from dark matter to dark energy, because such couplings make dark matter dilute faster than $a^{-3}$ and the ratio would drift. The equation work must choose between a fixed-share leak and a transfer before the test.
- **The ratio's value is a separate, registered test (T2):** with $f_{\rm leak}$ fixed from the expansion history (Section 2.4), the predicted $6(1 - f_{\rm leak})$ is checked against Planck 2018's $\omega_c/\omega_b$ with a tolerance set in advance.
- **Relevant data:** Planck 2018, DESI DR2 BAO, Pantheon+, DES SN 5-year, a BBN baryon density.
- **Status:** untested in v1.

## P4. Shadow offsets in merging clusters, with an age cutoff

- **Claim (Tier 3):** the shadow lags by a fixed $\tau_{\rm lag}$ and carries its source's motion.
- **The model expects:**
  1. **Young mergers** (time since pericenter $t_p < \tau_{\rm lag}$): the dark mass stays with the galaxies, and, because the shadow keeps its pre-collision speed while the real galaxies decelerate somewhat, it sits slightly *ahead* of the galaxies along the merger axis, by more the older (up to $\tau_{\rm lag}$) and faster the collision. Standard collisionless dark matter predicts no systematic offset from the galaxies; self-interacting dark matter predicts a small offset *behind* the galaxies, toward the gas. The sign is the discriminator.
  2. **Old mergers** ($t_p > \tau_{\rm lag}$): the news has arrived. The shadow of the gas lands on the gas and the shadow of the galaxies on the galaxies, so the lensing mass should split between gas and galaxies in roughly the proportion of their baryonic masses. Old mergers should lose the galaxy-centered offset. A fixed lag means a sharp age cutoff.
- **Would falsify:** (a) a well-measured merger with $t_p$ clearly greater than any allowed $\tau_{\rm lag}$ whose dark mass still tracks the galaxies with no shift toward the gas; together with the Bullet Cluster's requirement $\tau_{\rm lag} > 150$ Myr this can squeeze the allowed window shut; (b) a statistically significant population offset of dark matter *behind* galaxies in young mergers; (c) no age dependence at all across a sample spanning young and old mergers.
- **Key systems:** the Bullet Cluster (about 0.1 to 0.2 Gyr since core passage; Markevitch et al. 2002); the Musket Ball, DLSCL J0916.2+2951 (at least 0.7 $\pm$ 0.2 Gyr since first pass-through; Dawson et al. 2012), a natural first check of the cutoff; MACS J0025.4-1222 and El Gordo; and Abell 520 (see Open Problem 4).
- **Relevant data:** Harvey et al. 2015 (72 collisions in 30 systems), its re-analysis by Wittman, Golovich & Dawson 2018, and the Merging Cluster Collaboration sample of 29 radio-relic mergers (Golovich et al. 2019a, 2019b), with dynamical ages from methods such as Dawson (2013).
- **Status:** untested in v1.

## P5. Halo widths are set by the refraction width $\sigma_{\rm ref}$

- **Claim (Tier 3):** halos are the neighbors' baryon distribution, spread by a kernel of width $\sigma_{\rm ref}$ (Section 2.3).
- **The model expects:** a single kernel (one width, or a width set by one stated rule) applied to each galaxy's observed baryons (and its environment) should reproduce flat outer rotation curves across a large sample. Because the shadow is built from baryons, the dark contribution should be tightly predictable from the baryon distribution, consistent with the observed radial acceleration relation (McGaugh, Lelli & Schombert 2016).
- **Would falsify:** no single $\sigma_{\rm ref}$ (or stated rule) fixed on one subset of galaxies can fit a held-out subset to the pre-registered quality; or the required kernel would make the shadow so wide that the dark-matter-dominated dwarfs and the dark-matter-poor galaxies cannot both be produced.
- **Relevant data:** SPARC, 175 disk galaxies with Spitzer 3.6 $\mu$m photometry and rotation curves (Lelli, McGaugh & Schombert 2016).
- **Status:** untested in v1.

## P6. A faint far-string contribution

- **Claim (Tier 3):** strings beyond the touching six add a pull $\epsilon_{\rm far}$, arriving mostly as smooth tension.
- **The model expects:** a small component of the dark sector that is smoother than halo dark matter. In the background expansion it is degenerate with $f_{\rm leak}$; it can only be separated through clustering (a slight mismatch between how much the dark sector gravitates in the background and how much clumps in structure).
- **Would falsify:** this prediction is weak, and the paper says so. A precise consistency between expansion-history and structure-growth measurements that leaves no room for any smooth component at the level the model requires would count against it once the model states that level. Until $\epsilon_{\rm far}$ is given a target, it cannot fail, which is a defect, not a strength.
- **Relevant data:** DESI DR2 BAO with growth measurements, Planck 2018, weak-lensing surveys.
- **Status:** untested in v1.

## P7. The Hubble tension is a local reading

- **Claim (Tier 3):** the dark universe is a local reading, because every place has its own neighbors; the local expansion rate can carry a local accent.
- **Background (Tier 1):** the local distance ladder gives $H_0 = 73.04 \pm 1.04$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$ (Riess et al. 2022), against $67.4 \pm 0.5$ inferred from Planck under $\Lambda$CDM (Planck Collaboration 2020), a difference of about 8%. A local underdensity on a scale of about 300 Mpc has been proposed as a live explanation (Keenan, Barger & Cowie 2013; Tier 2).
- **The model expects:** the locally measured expansion rate depends on where and in which direction it is measured, at a level that can account for part or all of the tension, while the sky-averaged early-universe value is unaffected.
- **Would falsify:** a resolution of the tension that is entirely systematic (for example in the distance ladder), with local measurements showing no dependence on direction or environment; or a global modification of early-universe physics that removes the tension everywhere at once with no local component.
- **Relevant data:** SH0ES, Pantheon+ (which shares its calibration release with SH0ES), Planck 2018, DESI DR2.
- **Status:** untested in v1.

## P8. Locality of differences

- **Claim (Tier 3, built on Tier 1 relativity and a Tier 2 reading of many-worlds):** differences between histories spread no faster than light: $r_{\rm diff} = c\,t_{\rm split}$.
- **The model expects:** no observable effect that requires a difference between histories to spread faster than light. In the dark sector, the touching neighbors' matter is identical to ours within their difference bubbles, so on scales inside those bubbles the shadow should trace our own baryons (spread only by $\sigma_{\rm ref}$ and shifted only by $\tau_{\rm lag}$).
- **Would falsify:** a dark matter distribution that requires the touching neighbors' matter to differ from ours on scales larger than any recently split history could allow; or any confirmed superluminal signaling.
- **Honest limit:** this is largely a consistency requirement. Its concrete tests run through P4, P5 and Open Problem 3.
- **Status:** untested in v1.

## P9. A small departure from standard quantum mechanics inside a rope

- **Claim (Tier 3):** neighbors inside a rope still influence each other faintly through the now, unlike decohered branches in standard quantum mechanics.
- **The model expects:** a deviation from standard quantum mechanics that is tiny, never permits faster-than-light signaling, and is in principle measurable.
- **Would falsify:** in v1 the model gives no size or form for the deviation, so it cannot yet be tested. Stating one (with Polchinski's 1991 constraints on nonlinear quantum mechanics as the starting point) is a task for a later version. Precision tests of linearity bound any such effect.
- **Status:** untested in v1.

# 4. Open problems

These are stated plainly. Several of them could sink the model.

1. **The Bullet Cluster lag is underived.** The rescue in Section 2.6 needs $\tau_{\rm lag} > 150$ Myr. Nothing in the model yet says why the crossing between strings should take that long, or why it should be the same everywhere. Until it is derived, the lag is a parameter chosen to survive one observation.
2. **$f_{\rm leak}$ is fitted, not derived.** "About one sixth" is the expected value, chosen to match the dark matter share; the value the model is held to is fitted from the expansion history (Section 2.4, test T2). It should come from something physical, such as the width of the voids between strings or the helix geometry. Until then the dark matter and dark energy columns of Section 2.4 are not evidence.
3. **DF2 and DF4.** The ultra-diffuse galaxies NGC 1052-DF2 and NGC 1052-DF4 appear to contain little or no dark matter (van Dokkum et al. 2018, 2019), and they are thought to have formed in a single "bullet-dwarf" collision about 8 Gyr ago (van Dokkum et al. 2022). Recently split neighbors would share those galaxies, so their shadow should give them dark matter. A lag of 100 to 200 Myr does not obviously help with an event 8 Gyr old. Whether a wide refraction kernel, the group environment, or something else can produce dark-matter-poor galaxies is unanswered, and it may be fatal.
4. **Abell 520.** Weak-lensing studies report a dark core near the X-ray gas, away from the galaxies (Mahdavi et al. 2007; Jee et al. 2012, 2014), while an independent reconstruction finds no such core (Clowe et al. 2012). If the core is real and the merger is older than $\tau_{\rm lag}$, it could support the age cutoff of P4 (the shadow of the gas would be expected on the gas). If the core is real and the merger is younger than $\tau_{\rm lag}$, it is a stress test the model currently fails. Its age and the reality of the core both have to be pinned down.
5. **The coincidence problem.** The 1:6:12 match only holds today, because dark energy's share keeps growing. A few hundred million years ago the numbers did not match this well, and a few hundred million years from now they will not. Why we live in the era when dark matter and dark energy are comparable is a recognized puzzle in standard cosmology (see for example Zlatev, Wang & Steinhardt 1999). The model inherits it and does not solve it: the numbers line up today, and nobody, the author included, knows why today.
6. **The starlight and handshake problem.** In the model's version of the transactional interpretation (Cramer 1986; Tier 2), confirmation waves can run backward only as far as the now. Light that left a star billions of years ago would need its reply to reach deep into settled history. Two leads exist and neither is finished: Ruth Kastner's possibilist version, in which the handshake happens in a realm of possibility outside ordinary spacetime (Kastner 2012), and the model's own proposal that the boundary at the end of each turn of the spiral acts as a final absorber, answering every offer still in flight.
7. **Known instabilities in interacting dark energy.** If the leak is written as an energy exchange between dark matter and a dark energy fluid, some of the simplest couplings produce a large-scale instability in the perturbations at early times (Valiviita, Majerotto & Maartens 2008). Any formal version of the leak must avoid these couplings or show why the model's version is different.
8. **Why our string sits at a strand's center.** The model puts us in the middle of a 1-6-12 strand. But every string is at the center of its own neighborhood only if strand membership is local and relative, which Section 1.8 suggests; otherwise most strings would sit in a ring, and their dark-sector budget would differ. Which reading holds, and whether it is consistent, is not worked out.
9. **No physical derivation of the $N_s$ geometry.** Six around one is forced for equal circles in a plane. Nothing yet says why histories should be equal "circles," why the packing is planar in the sideways directions, or why the strand stops at two rings. $N_s = 19$ and $N_r = 133$ are, for now, assumptions borrowed from geometry and rope.
10. **The ratio tension.** The leak cannot match both the shares and the ratio. Near one sixth it matches the dark matter and dark energy shares, but it predicts a dark-to-ordinary ratio of about 5.0 against Planck's 5.36. Matching Planck's ratio would need $f_{\rm leak} \approx 0.107$, well below the expected one sixth, and that value then misses the shares. Version 1.0.1 does not resolve this. It registers a single role for the leak (fixed from the expansion history, tested on the ratio, Section 2.4) so that the tension is tested rather than tuned away, and T2 is expected to fail unless the expansion fit lands well below one sixth.
11. **DESI's rise-then-fall dark energy history.** T1 tests only $w_0$, today's value. The DESI DR2 best-fit CPL curves say more: at higher redshift the equation of state sits below $-1$, more negative than a cosmological constant's (a phantom phase, in which the dark energy density grows with time); it crosses $w = -1$ at intermediate redshift (roughly $z \approx 0.4$ to 0.5); and today it sits above $-1$, so the inferred density rose, peaked and is now falling (DESI Collaboration 2025b). A steadily unwinding strand does not obviously allow the rising phase: monotonic unwinding gives $w \geq -1$ at all times (see P1's open sub-question and the equation sheet's unwinding section). Whether the leak's coupling can make the effective $w$ look phantom in the past, or whether the model must forbid the crossing and bet against it, is unanswered.

# Appendix B. Estropy

This appendix is in three labeled parts: a historical source (an unpublished paper, quoted verbatim), the physics context (Tier 1 and Tier 2), and the author's personal belief (**philosophy, not physics**).

## B.1 The source

The concept of Estropy comes from an unpublished paper by the author's father, Carmine Mandarano, titled *On the Quantum Thermodynamics of Life: The God Receptor*. It is dedicated to David Rubin. Quotations below are verbatim, with the original spelling and punctuation.

The paper's question is how complex life arose given the second law of thermodynamics. Of the odds, it says: "There is something missing." Its proposal:

> "I therefore postulate that there exists in the universe mind energy that permeates the universe this mind energy I call Estropy."

> "It cannot be created nor destroyed it merely IS."

> "Thus as the entropy of the universe increases, the decreased entropy of biologic systems that occurs naturally is balanced precisely by an increase in the Estropy."

The paper gives the relation, in its own notation, as "D (entropy)/dt = - D(Estropy)/dt for biologic entities (life forms)." In standard notation, with $S$ the entropy of a living system and $E_s$ its Estropy:

$$
\frac{dS}{dt} \;=\; -\,\frac{dE_s}{dt} \qquad \text{(for biologic entities)} .
$$

It continues: "Intelligent life evolves not despite the 2nd Law (of Thermodynamics) but because of it", and "Life must occur without hindrance as certain as thermal equilibrium or water flowing down hill". It ends: "Life finds a way driven by the arrow of Estopy and time." (spelling as in the original).

## B.2 The physics context

- **Negative entropy (Tier 1 history).** Schrödinger (1944) argued that a living organism maintains its order by feeding on "negative entropy" from its environment.
- **Dissipation-driven adaptation (Tier 2).** England (2013) derived a generalization of the second law for self-replicating systems driven far from equilibrium, and has argued that matter driven by an external energy source tends to organize in ways that absorb and dissipate that energy, which would make the emergence of life-like organization less surprising.
- **The standard resolution (Tier 1).** Most physicists see no conflict to resolve. A living thing is an open system: it lowers its own entropy by exporting more entropy to its surroundings, so the total entropy still rises and the second law holds without any new kind of energy. Estropy has never been measured, and physics can account for life without it.

The paper's relation can be read as one way of writing the bookkeeping of an open system, with Estropy standing where standard thermodynamics places the entropy exported to the environment. It is not part of established physics.

## B.3 The author's belief (philosophy, not physics)

**This section is belief. It is not physics, not part of the model's testable content, and has no supporting evidence and no known test.**

The author believes that Estropy comes from minds older and larger than ours: that the consciousness of planets, of stars, and of the black holes at the centers of galaxies accounts for it. Philosophers call the view that mind is a fundamental and pervasive feature of matter **panpsychism**; the view that the cosmos as a whole is conscious, and that smaller minds derive from it, is called **cosmopsychism** (both surveyed in the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry "Panpsychism"). The author's version extends these to the largest bodies in the universe.

In that belief, the model's last open question (who winds the strand back up for the next turn of the spiral) has an answer: in the far future the black holes are the last minds left, they wind the strand, and then, one by one, they evaporate, as Hawking radiation predicts, leaving the universe of pure light that Penrose's cyclic cosmology treats as the seed of the next. The physics in that sentence is a standard but unobserved theoretical prediction (black hole evaporation) and Tier 2 speculation (Penrose's cycles); the minds and the winding are belief only. Nothing in this appendix makes any religious claim beyond the title of Carmine Mandarano's paper.
