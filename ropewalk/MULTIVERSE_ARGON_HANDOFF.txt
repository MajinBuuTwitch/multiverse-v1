B1. THE MODEL IN BRIEF (Tier 3 unless marked)
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Status: a model, not a theory. Not peer reviewed. Written to be broken.

Structure.
- Each complete history of our universe is a STRING. The present is a fixed "blade" (the model promotes cosmological cosmic time, Tier 1 bookkeeping, to a physical preferred present, by analogy with the Lorentz and Poincare preferred-frame versions of relativity; it is built to reproduce every measured relativistic effect). Histories are coils that turn through the fixed blade.
- Strings are held under tension. Changing the past "plucks" a string; small plucks heal (elastic), large ones bend history for good (plastic, past a yield point), the largest snap it. These time-travel mechanics have no cosmological observable and are not part of this job.
- Histories that split recently stay close and in step ("sync"); decoherence pushes them apart. The twist of a strand sets the balance.
- STRAND: 19 strings, N_s = 1 + 6 + 12 = 19 (ours at the center, six touching it, twelve around those; the six-around-one packing is forced by geometry). ROPE: 7 strands, N_r = 7 x 19 = 133. CABLE: all ropes. There are gaps ("voids") at every level: small gaps between strings, larger ones between strands, and THE Void between ropes, which is uncrossable.
- Many-worlds' countless branches are mostly fine fuzz on each string; only a large difference makes a string of its own.
- Dimension ladder (narrative only): 0D to 7D are our universe under our laws; 8D is the cable as a point; 9D to 11D are other cables with different laws.
- LOCALITY: differences between histories spread no faster than light. A split of age dt differs only inside a bubble of radius about c dt. So the six touching neighbors, which split recently, contain the same matter in the same places as ours on galaxy scales. Every place has its own six neighbors, so the dark universe is a local reading.

The dark sector mapping.
- DARK MATTER = THE SHADOW. Gravity crosses mainly where strings touch. The six touching neighbors' baryons pull on our universe; that pull is dark matter. It never collides with anything here because it is not here.
- REFRACTION: crossing the small void between strings spreads the pull, so it lands as a wide halo (width sigma_ref).
- THE LAG: the crossing takes time tau_lag, and the shadow carries its source's motion, pointing to where the source would be had it kept its velocity (analogy: in general relativity the field of a uniformly moving mass points at its current extrapolated position, Carlip 2000, Tier 1). In the Bullet Cluster the gas stopped, but its shadow, sent before the crash, kept going with the galaxies. Pure lag without carried motion puts the mass on the wrong side and fails. Required: tau_lag greater than about 150 Myr. Underived.
- THE LEAK: about a sixth (f_leak) of the six neighbors' pull is lost in transit into the voids and is stored as tension in the outer twelve. It must raise dark energy's SHARE, not make dark energy density RISE over time (that would fight the weakening below). One fitted number: since shares sum to 100 percent, it fixes the dark matter and dark energy gaps together by construction. "About a sixth" is the EXPECTED value, not a fitted one. f_leak has ONE pre-registered role: it is FIXED from the expansion history (DESI DR2 BAO + compressed CMB priors + supernovae, with a BBN baryon density), and the dark-to-ordinary ratio it then predicts, 6 (1 - f_leak), is TESTED against Planck 2018 (T2).
- FAR STRINGS: every other string adds a faint pull (epsilon_far), mostly arriving as smooth tension after more crossings and more leak. A weak prediction with no target yet.
- DARK ENERGY = OUTER-RING TENSION, not mass (Tier 1 fact underneath: a cosmological constant behaves as a tension, p = -rho c^2).
- THE TWIST: the strand is slowly unwinding, losing tension, so dark energy should be weakening (w0 > -1, wa < 0), as DESI hinted in 2024 and 2025. If future data say constant forever, the model loses this claim. The beginning was maximum twist; the arrow of time points from wound to unwound (a picture, not a solution). A fully unwound strand is rewound for the next cycle (analogy: Steinhardt and Turok cyclic model).
- PREDICTIONS the job touches: dark energy weakening; the dark-to-ordinary ratio fixed over cosmic time; offsets in merging clusters with an age cutoff at tau_lag; halo widths set by sigma_ref; no dark matter particle in direct detection (not part of this job); the Hubble tension as a local reading (report only).

Open problems the model states plainly (do not try to hide them; flag where your work bears on them): the lag is underived; f_leak is fitted, not derived; NGC 1052-DF2 and DF4 (dark-matter-poor galaxies that recently split neighbors should share, so their shadow should give them dark matter); Abell 520 (a disputed dark core at the gas); the coincidence (the shares line up only today); known instabilities of interacting dark energy; no physical derivation of the N_s geometry; the ratio tension (the leak cannot match both the shares and the ratio; see the paper's open problems); DESI's rise-then-fall dark energy history (a phantom past, a crossing of w = -1, weakening today), which a steadily unwinding strand does not obviously allow.

B2. THE EQUATION SHEET (inline, plain text)

Conventions: flat space; a = scale factor, a = 1 today, 1+z = 1/a; t = cosmic time; dot = d/dt; H = (da/dt)/a; Omega_i = rho_i0 / rho_crit0, rho_crit0 = 3 H0^2 / (8 pi G); omega_i = Omega_i h^2. Subscripts: b baryons; dm the shadow; de tension (dark energy); r radiation.
Shared parameter names (identical in every file): N_s, N_r, f_leak, epsilon_far, w0, wa, tau_lag, sigma_ref.

(a) BACKGROUND COSMOLOGY (Tier 1 equations; Tier 3 mapping)
a1 [established] ds^2 = -c^2 dt^2 + a(t)^2 [dr^2 + r^2 dOmega^2].
a2 [established] H^2 = (8 pi G / 3) sum_i rho_i; H(a)^2 / H0^2 = Omega_r a^-4 + (Omega_b + Omega_dm) a^-3 + Omega_de g(a), with g(a) = rho_de(a)/rho_de0 and sum Omega_i = 1. The a^-3 for dark matter holds only if it is uncoupled.
a3 [established] Each uncoupled fluid: d(rho_i)/dt + 3 H (1 + w_i) rho_i = 0.
a4 [established] CPL (Chevallier and Polarski 2001; Linder 2003): w(a) = w0 + wa (1 - a); uncoupled g(a) = a^(-3(1 + w0 + wa)) exp(-3 wa (1 - a)). LambdaCDM is w0 = -1, wa = 0.
a5 [established] D_M(z) = c Int_0^z dz'/H(z'); D_H = c/H(z); D_V = (z D_M^2 D_H)^(1/3); D_L = (1+z) D_M; mu = 5 log10(D_L / 10 pc); r_d = Int_{z_d}^inf c_s(z)/H(z) dz. BAO data give D_M/r_d, D_H/r_d, D_V/r_d. Compressed CMB priors (Chen, Huang and Wang 2019) use R = sqrt(Omega_m H0^2) D_M(z*)/c, l_A = pi D_M(z*)/r_s(z*), and omega_b, with their published covariance. They assume standard pre-recombination physics; check that assumption before using them for any model that changes early-time dark matter. The f_leak fix (c4) uses only R and l_A from these priors, with omega_b from a BBN prior instead.
a6 [model ansatz] Background components: baryons; the shadow (pressureless, w = 0, in the static form of b5); tension (dark energy, described by w0, wa or by a coupled fluid).
Reference values [Tier 1], Planck 2018 base LambdaCDM, TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing (Planck Collaboration 2020, Table 2): omega_b = 0.02237 +/- 0.00015; omega_c = 0.1200 +/- 0.0012; H0 = 67.36 +/- 0.54 km/s/Mpc; Omega_m = 0.3153 +/- 0.0073; Omega_Lambda = 0.6847 +/- 0.0073. So Omega_b about 0.049, Omega_c about 0.265, omega_c/omega_b about 5.36.

(b) THE LEAK AS INTERACTING DARK ENERGY (Tier 1 conservation; Tier 2 couplings; Tier 3 link)
b1 [established] Sign convention here: Q > 0 means energy flows FROM dark matter TO dark energy (the leak's direction).
    d(rho_dm)/dt + 3 H rho_dm = -Q
    d(rho_de)/dt + 3 H (1 + w) rho_de = +Q
    d(rho_b)/dt + 3 H rho_b = 0
    Papers differ: Gavela et al. 2009 put +Q on the dark matter side (their Q = minus ours); Boehmer et al. 2008 use our direction.
b2 [established] Effective equations of state (signs adapted from Gavela et al. 2009, Eqs. 5 and 6): w_dm_eff = Q / (3 H rho_dm); w_de_eff = w - Q / (3 H rho_de). Energy flowing into dark energy lowers its effective w; an analysis that ignores the coupling can infer w < -1 even when the true w > -1 (Das, Corasaniti and Khoury 2006).
b3 [established forms; Tier 2 as physics]
    Q-dm:    Q = xi_m H rho_dm   (Boehmer et al. 2008 model II; xi_m > 0 is dark matter decaying to dark energy). Integrates to rho_dm proportional to a^(-3 - xi_m), so rho_dm/rho_b drifts as a^(-xi_m).
    Q-de:    Q = xi_e H rho_de   (Gavela et al. 2009 write Q = xi H rho_de with the opposite sign: xi_e = -xi).
    Q-rate:  Q = Gamma rho_dm, constant Gamma (Boehmer et al. 2008 model III; Valiviita, Majerotto and Maartens 2008).
    Q-field: Q proportional to beta rho_dm dphi/dt, coupled quintessence (Amendola 2000).
    Review: Wang, Abdalla, Atrio-Barandela and Pavon 2016.
b4 [established, known problems]
    - Valiviita, Majerotto and Maartens 2008: constant-w fluid dark energy with these simple couplings has an early-time, large-scale (super-Hubble) instability in the curvature perturbation, however weak the coupling.
    - Gavela et al. 2009: the instability is governed by the sign of the "doom factor" d = Q_G / (3 H rho_de (1 + w)) in their convention (Q_G = -Q here); unstable when d is positive and large. For Q-de there is an instability-free region with (their notation) xi < 0 and w > -1, which in our convention is energy flowing from dark matter to dark energy with w > -1: the model's direction.
    - For couplings proportional to rho_dm, the ratio rho_dm/rho_de makes |d| large at early times even for tiny couplings, and Gavela et al. note that with energy flowing from dark matter to dark energy, rho_de turns negative in the past.
    - Consequence: the form the narration suggests (a fixed fraction of the shadow's pull, so Q proportional to rho_dm) is the problematic one; Q-de has a published stable branch in the model's direction. This is an open choice for Stage 1.
b5 [model ansatz] One definition of f_leak, two candidate mechanisms. f_leak has a single definition and a single pre-registered role: it is the parameter of today's shares (c3), fixed from the expansion history and tested on the ratio (c4). What the model has not chosen is the mechanism:
    Static form (transit efficiency; no energy transfer in the background):
        rho_dm(a) = (1 - f_leak) N_in rho_b(a), N_in = 6.
        The ratio stays fixed at all times at 6 (1 - f_leak), the value T2 tests. How the leaked part becomes non-diluting tension is unexplained bookkeeping (a Stage 1 task).
    Transfer form (dynamic transfer through Q from b3): the coupling must reproduce today's shares with the fixed f_leak. The transferred fraction of the shadow's energy since a_i is
        1 - rho_dm(1) / (rho_dm(a_i) a_i^3) = [1 / (rho_dm(a_i) a_i^3)] Int_{t_i}^{t0} Q a^3 dt,
        which for Q-dm with constant xi_m is 1 - a_i^(xi_m). The start epoch a_i is an assumption to state. The transfer form breaks the fixed ratio of (c) at a level set by the coupling (for Q-de, mostly at late times).
    Model constraint: in the transfer form, d(rho_de)/dt = -3 H (1 + w) rho_de + Q must stay negative or near zero today (share grows, density does not rise).

Note: the cancellation is for uniform motion; extrapolating accelerated sources is a further assumption.
e3 [model ansatz] rho_sh(x, t) = (1 - f_leak) sum_{k=1..6} Int d^3y K_sigma(x - y) rhotilde_b^(k)(y, t)
    rhotilde_b^(k)(y, t) = sum_j m_j^(k) delta^3( y - [ x_j^(k)(t - tau_lag) + v_j^(k)(t - tau_lag) tau_lag ] )
    (j runs over baryonic mass elements of neighbor k.)
e4 [model ansatz] Locality: rho_b^(k) = rho_b for all six, so rho_sh = 6 (1 - f_leak) [K_sigma convolved with rhotilde_b].
e5 [model ansatz] Default kernel: K_sigma(r) = (2 pi sigma_ref^2)^(-3/2) exp(-|r|^2 / (2 sigma_ref^2)), normalized to 1, so the total shadow mass is 6 (1 - f_leak) times the baryonic mass. Physical vs comoving width, and any scaling with the source, are open (see f). Precedent [Tier 2]: nonlocal gravity sourced by an integral transform of ordinary matter (Hehl and Mashhoon 2009).
e6 [model ansatz] Merging clusters, 1D along the merger axis. S = stars/galaxies, G = gas, I = lensing-inferred mass (labels of Harvey et al. 2015). A symmetric kernel and linear extrapolation preserve centroids, so the shadow of component c has centroid
    xtilde_c(t) = x_c(t - tau_lag) + v_c(t - tau_lag) tau_lag, c in {S, G}
    x_I(t) = [ M_S x_S + M_G x_G + 6 (1 - f_leak) (M_S xtilde_S + M_G xtilde_G) ] / ( [1 + 6 (1 - f_leak)] (M_S + M_G) )
    Observables: delta_SG = x_G - x_S; delta_SI = x_I - x_S; beta = delta_SI / delta_SG. Lensing analyses measure peaks, not centroids; handle that difference explicitly.
e7 [model ansatz] T = time since first pericenter.
    Young (T < tau_lag): both shadows are extrapolated from before the collision; the gas's shadow did not stop. The project's stated expectation: the shadow keeps its pre-crash speed and sits slightly AHEAD of the galaxies, more so the older and faster the collision (up to T = tau_lag), so beta <= 0. The sign depends on the infall history (velocities at t - tau_lag may be lower than at pericenter): compute it with a dynamical merger model, do not assume it.
    Old (T > tau_lag): if xtilde_G = x_G and xtilde_S = x_S, then beta -> M_G / (M_S + M_G), the baryonic gas fraction: the mass snaps back onto the gas. Collisionless cold dark matter predicts beta about 0 at all ages.
    A fixed tau_lag is an AGE CUTOFF.
Targets: tau_lag greater than about 150 Myr (Bullet Cluster core passage 0.1 to 0.2 Gyr ago, Markevitch et al. 2002; its mass sits with the galaxies, Clowe et al. 2006). Ensemble: dark mass within 5.8 +/- 8.2 kpc of the stars over 72 collisions in 30 systems (Harvey et al. 2015), systematics revisited by Wittman, Golovich and Dawson 2018. Dawson 2013: the Musket Ball is further progressed than the Bullet by at least about 0.8 Gyr (large uncertainty). Abell 520: dark core near the gas reported by Jee et al. 2014, not detected by Clowe et al. 2012; in the model a peak at the gas is natural only for T > tau_lag, and then the galaxies should carry correspondingly less mass.

(f) ROTATION CURVES (Tier 1 for f1, f2; Tier 3 for f3, f4)
f1 [established] v_c(r)^2 = G [M_b(<r) + M_sh(<r)] / r (spherical approximation). Flat curve over a range: total M(<r) proportional to r there.
f2 [established, empirical] Radial acceleration relation (McGaugh, Lelli and Schombert 2016; 2693 points in 153 SPARC galaxies): g_obs = g_bar / (1 - exp(-sqrt(g_bar / g_dagger))), g_dagger = 1.20 +/- 0.02 (random) +/- 0.24 (systematic) x 10^-10 m/s^2.
f3 [model ansatz] g_obs = g_bar + g_sh, with g_sh from e4 and e5 applied to each galaxy's measured baryons (SPARC mass models). Targets: (1) flat outer curves; (2) the radial acceleration relation with ONE rule for sigma_ref; (3) the cap: with a normalized kernel an isolated galaxy's shadow totals 6 (1 - f_leak) M_b, about 5 M_b, so beyond saturation v_c falls as r^(-1/2); SPARC's low-acceleration points have g_obs/g_bar above 6; check whether the cap allows that, or whether overlapping shadows, local variation of R, or a different kernel is needed.
f4 [model ansatz] Carried motion on a circular orbit: shadow radius r_ext = r sqrt(1 + (v_c tau_lag / r)^2) > r. Conversion: 1 km/s for 1 Myr is about 1.02 pc. Evaluate v_c tau_lag for disks with tau_lag above about 150 Myr and decide whether it helps or ruins halo shapes.
Candidate extensions (declare before fitting, or do not use): sigma_ref = s R_d (R_d = disk scale length, s dimensionless); a non-Gaussian kernel.

(g) LOCALITY (Tier 1 for g1; Tier 2 receipt; Tier 3 for g2 to g4)
g1 [established] Proper radius reached by light emitted at t_s: R_light(t; t_s) = a(t) Int_{t_s}^{t} c dt' / a(t'), about c (t - t_s) when expansion is negligible.
g2 [model ansatz] Histories split at t_s by an event at x_s differ only where |x - x_s| <= R_light(t; t_s). Receipt [Tier 2]: Deutsch and Hayden 2000 (many-worlds described locally).
g3 [model ansatz] Sideways distance tracks the age of a split: recent = strand-mates; older = across the strand gaps; oldest = other ropes. Small bubbles justify e4.
g4 [model ansatz] Local reading: local H0 = 73.04 +/- 1.04 (Riess et al. 2022) vs 67.36 +/- 0.54 (Planck 2018), about 8 percent, read as a local accent (compatible with the published local-void idea). No equation in v1.

(h) THE COINCIDENCE (Tier 1 for h1; open problem for the model)
h1 [established] Omega_de(a) = Omega_de g(a) / (Omega_r a^-4 + Omega_m a^-3 + Omega_de g(a)). In LambdaCDM, rho_dm/rho_de is proportional to a^-3, so the dark shares are comparable only in a limited era that includes today.
h2 [model statement] The 1:6:12 shares (with the leak) match only near today; the model has no mechanism that selects today. Task: with Planck 2018 parameters, compute the span of cosmic time during which all three shares stay within a tolerance declared in PREREG.md of the section c values; report it without tuning.
Tier 2 note: interacting dark energy has been proposed as a route to the coincidence problem (late-time attractors with constant dark matter to dark energy ratio; Boehmer et al. 2008; Wang et al. 2016). A possible lead, not a claim.

These rules and thresholds are copied from the public file MULTIVERSE_TESTS_v1.md, version 1.0.1 (in this same folder). They were set before any data were examined. GENERAL RULES
G1. No tuning after seeing the test data. No parameter, kernel shape, sample cut, error model or criterion may change after a test dataset has been loaded; a change voids the test.
G2. Fix on one dataset, test on another. A dataset used to fix a parameter is never used to test that same parameter. One partial overlap is registered and stated plainly: in T2, the Planck CMB enters the fix only through the compressed distance priors (R, l_A) and the test only through omega_c / omega_b (see the T2 independence caveat).
G3. Freeze before testing. Before a test dataset is opened, write the fixed values (with uncertainties), the analysis code version and the date to a freeze file (FREEZE_<test>.json), and record its SHA-256. For the code version, use a git commit hash if git is available in the project folder; otherwise the SHA-256 of a zip of scripts/. The test run must load the frozen values from that file. (PREREG.md from R4 records all of this together.)
G4. Thresholds are fixed. Three outcomes, all reported: PASS, FAIL, INCONCLUSIVE.
G5. Structural constants are not free: N_s = 19 and N_r = 133 may not be varied.
G6. No double counting. Pantheon+ and DES SN are never combined in one fit; every supernova test is run separately with each, and both results are reported. Planck's omega_b and omega_c are used only to test f_leak (T2), never to fix it; the T2 fix may use Planck only through the compressed distance priors R and l_A. Planck also serves as the CMB prior in T1, a test of a different parameter.
G7. "n sigma" means the two-sided Gaussian-equivalent significance, computed with each dataset's published likelihood and covariance.
G8. Ambiguities count as of the freeze: if a prediction is ambiguous, resolve it in writing in PREREG.md before the freeze, or that part of the test is INCONCLUSIVE.
G9. Validation before any test run: the background code must reproduce a published LambdaCDM result from the same data (for example the DESI DR2 BAO-only Omega_m stated in DESI Collaboration 2025b) to within 0.3 sigma. MCMC runs must converge (Gelman-Rubin R - 1 below 0.01, or effective sample size above 1000 per parameter); an unconverged run is INCONCLUSIVE, not PASS.

T1. DARK ENERGY WEAKENING (w0, wa)
  Prediction fixed in advance: w0 > -1 (dark energy density falling today). v1 does not say whether w < -1 was allowed in the past, so only w0 is tested. DESI's rise-then-fall dark energy history (a phantom past and a crossing of w = -1) is not tested here; it is listed in the paper's open problems.
  FIX: none fitted. Planck 2018 enters only as the standard CMB prior (compressed or full, as used by the DESI DR2 analysis).
  TEST: DESI DR2 BAO and DESI DR2 Lyman-alpha (including the Alcock-Paczynski constraints of arXiv:2607.27410), plus Pantheon+ in one run and DES SN 5-year, DES-Dovekie version, in a second run. Flat w0waCDM.
  PASS: in both supernova runs, w0 > -1 at 95 percent credibility (marginalized over wa and the standard parameters).
  FAIL: in either run, w0 < -1 at 95 percent credibility; or, in any future release where the 68 percent uncertainty on w0 is 0.03 or smaller, (w0, wa) = (-1, 0) lies inside the 68 percent region in both supernova runs.
  INCONCLUSIVE: anything else.
  Note: if the Lyman-alpha Alcock-Paczynski data products are not public, use the published constraint as described in the paper and state exactly how; if that is impossible, run without it and record the deviation.

T2. THE LEAK AND THE DARK-TO-ORDINARY RATIO (f_leak)
  Parameter: f_leak, with model shares s_b = 1/19, s_dm = 6 (1 - f_leak) / 19, s_de = (12 + 6 f_leak) / 19. One pre-registered role: fixed from the expansion history, then tested on the dark-to-ordinary ratio. "About one sixth" is the expected value, not a fitted one.
  FIX: DESI DR2 BAO + the compressed CMB distance priors + supernovae, with a BBN baryon density, in flat w0waCDM (the background of T1) with the sum of neutrino masses fixed at 0.06 eV. Declared now:
    - the CMB enters only through the shift parameter R and the acoustic scale l_A: the (R, l_A) block of the Planck 2018 distance priors of Chen, Huang and Wang 2019 (JCAP 02 (2019) 028), marginalized over their omega_b;
    - omega_b comes from the BBN prior of Cooke, Pettini and Steidel 2018 (ApJ 855, 102): 100 omega_b = 2.166 +/- 0.019 (their headline value, statistical and BBN-calculation errors of 0.015 and 0.011 added in quadrature). Planck's omega_b and omega_c are not used anywhere in the fix;
    - the fit is run separately with Pantheon+ and with DES SN 5-year (DES-Dovekie version), never combined (G6);
    - from the marginalized posterior of the total matter density today, Omega_m (median and 68 percent half-width): f_leak = 1 - (19 Omega_m - 1) / 6 and sigma_f = 19 sigma_Omega_m / 6. At exactly one sixth, Omega_m = 6/19, about 0.316.
  TEST: Planck 2018 base LambdaCDM, TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing (Planck Collaboration 2020, Table 2): omega_b = 0.02237 +/- 0.00015, omega_c = 0.1200 +/- 0.0012, so R_Planck = omega_c / omega_b = 5.36 +/- 0.065 (relative errors added in quadrature, the correlation between the two ignored; this value and uncertainty are fixed now).
  Prediction: R_pred = 6 (1 - f_leak), equivalently 19 Omega_m - 1, with uncertainty 6 sigma_f.
  PASS: |R_pred - R_Planck| <= 2 sigma_comb, where sigma_comb = sqrt((6 sigma_f)^2 + sigma_Planck^2) and sigma_Planck = 0.065, in both supernova runs.
  FAIL: otherwise (|R_pred - R_Planck| > 2 sigma_comb in either supernova run).
  INCONCLUSIVE: only for insufficient data (a fixing run whose code fails to reproduce a published LambdaCDM result from the same data, or whose sampling does not converge; see G9). T2 has no threshold region between PASS and FAIL.
  Stated in advance, honestly: at f_leak = 1/6 the prediction is 6 x 5/6 = 5.0, which sits about 5.6 sigma below Planck's 5.36 counting Planck's uncertainty alone. The combined sigma also contains 6 sigma_f, so the final margin depends on how tightly the expansion fit pins f_leak, but this test is expected to FAIL unless the expansion fit lands well below one sixth. This is the ratio tension listed in the paper's open problems. A FAIL here does not touch the other tests, but it would mean the leak, in its v1 form, does not work.
  Independence caveat: R and l_A are derived from the same Planck maps as the test's omega_c / omega_b, and R depends on the matter density, so the fix and the test are not fully independent. Keeping Planck's omega_b and omega_c out of the fix (BBN supplies omega_b) reduces the overlap; it does not remove it.
  Downstream use: T5 uses the f_leak frozen in the Pantheon+ run (declared now).
  Forbidden: f_leak has no other fix and no alternative value. It may not be refitted to the ratio or fixed on Planck's omega_b or omega_c, and epsilon_far may not be introduced to absorb a T2 failure, unless such a change is registered in a later version and tested on new data.
