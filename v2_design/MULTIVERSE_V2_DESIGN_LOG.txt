ENTRY 1 · 2026-10-31 · Gregory Mandarano (planning-chat critique noted)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IDEA: V1's 1/19 count was too macro. Each string (timeline) has an unbounded number of adjacent neighbours, as
many-worlds implies, not just six. "Frays" are branches too small to become their own timeline. In v2, frays are
QUANTUM BRANCHING EVENTS (not conscious choices), and they carry weight that reaches neighbouring timelines as dark
matter. Strings need not carry the same number of frays, so the dark-matter share can differ from place to place.
DESIGN TARGETS AND KNOWN PROBLEMS (planning chat):
 - Infinite neighbours need a falloff with "distance" between timelines so the total converges to a finite,
   possibly non-integer effective count (~20.3 if the shares are to match). The falloff must be DERIVED, not chosen
   to hit 20.3 (charter R2).
 - Dark matter is present at full strength by recombination (CMB), so frays must exist from the start; quantum
   branching satisfies this, conscious choices do not.
 - Galaxies' dark matter tracks their visible matter tightly (the radial acceleration relation). Fray counts must
   therefore correlate with baryon content or acceleration, not vary randomly. This is the decisive design test.

ENTRY 2 · 2026-10-31 · Gregory Mandarano
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IDEA: Dark energy tracks the universe's branching activity: it rose after the Big Bang, peaked around "cosmic noon"
(the peak of cosmic star formation, z ≈ 2, about 10 billion years ago) and has weakened since. When it reaches
zero, or a breaking point, the timeline ends: a crunch, a collapse of the timeline, or a start-over.
IMPLICATIONS AND CHECKS (planning chat):
 - A dark-energy density that rises and then falls means w < −1 before the peak and w > −1 after: a crossing of
   w = −1 AT THE PEAK. This predicts the crossing near z ≈ 2. (v1's Law 1 forbade crossing; v1's exploratory
   transfer coupling allowed an effective one.) Commonly used two-parameter fits to current data place a crossing
   much later (roughly z ≈ 0.4–0.5), although that parametrization forces a particular shape: a real point of
   tension to test, not to tune away.
 - Amplitude: dark energy cannot have been many times denser at z ≈ 2 than today; expansion-history data constrain
   it to stay close to today's level. If tied to star formation (which has fallen roughly tenfold since its peak),
   it must be a small modulation on a steady component, not proportional to the star-formation rate.
 - Ending: if dark energy only reaches zero, a flat universe keeps expanding forever while slowing. A crunch needs
   dark energy to turn negative. "Start-over" connects to published cyclic cosmologies (Tier 2) and to the
   documentary's ending.
 - Mechanism needed: why branching activity, and why star formation specifically, sets dark energy.

ENTRY 3 · 2026-10-31 · Gregory Mandarano (planning-chat critique noted)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IDEA (revises the timing in Entry 2): Dark energy, branching and expansion are all at their MAXIMUM at the Big Bang.
Gravity then slows the expansion; once matter is spread thin it speeds up again; later it slows again because dark
energy has been falling. Branching falls as the universe spreads out, because there is less interaction: less
interaction, fewer branches, fewer frays, less dark energy. NEGATIVE dark energy = the strand fraying INWARD: the
timeline loses cohesion and holds fewer possibilities every day. At a breaking point the timeline SNAPS: the Big
Crunch, everything collapses to a 0D point, then bursts apart into fresh timelines with maximum dark energy (a cycle).
DESIGN TARGETS AND KNOWN PROBLEMS (planning chat):
 - Two versions now exist: Entry 2 (dark energy peaks at cosmic noon) and Entry 3 (dark energy is maximum at the Big
   Bang). They can be combined (an inflation-scale spike, near zero through the early eras, then a later bump), but
   v2 must pick one. Constraint: during the radiation and matter eras dark energy must have been a tiny fraction of
   the total (CMB and nucleosynthesis limits). If "maximum at the start" means inflation, inflation's energy density
   was more than ~10^100 times today's dark energy: one mechanism must span that whole range.
 - Physics wording to fix: the early slowdown came from radiation and matter (mostly dark matter), not stars (the
   first stars formed ~100–200 million years in). The universe was thinning, not getting "thicker", while slowing.
 - Interaction → branching: decoherence (quantum branching) continues wherever particles or photons scatter,
   including the CMB, so it does not obviously fall as space spreads out. "Number of branches" is not a well-defined
   quantity in many-worlds. v2 needs a measurable proxy for branching rate (e.g. cosmic entropy production rate, star
   formation rate, or interaction rate) chosen and DERIVED before fenced data are seen (charter R2). Precedent (Tier
   2): Bousso, Harnik, Kribs & Perez 2007, the causal entropic principle, links entropy production to the VALUE of
   the cosmological constant (a selection argument, not a time-varying one).
 - Sign flip: "fewer branches" naturally gives LESS positive dark energy, not negative. A candidate form (planner's
   suggestion, not Gregory's): dark energy ∝ (branching rate − a fixed cohesion cost of the strand). It goes negative
   once branching falls below the cohesion cost. Any such baseline counts against the knob budget (R1).
 - Two jobs for one object: in Entry 1, frays carry dark-matter weight (clumps, no pressure); here they also set dark
   energy (smooth, negative pressure). The model must say how one population behaves both ways, or split them.
 - Cycle: published cyclic and bouncing cosmologies exist (Tier 2: Steinhardt–Turok cyclic, loop-quantum-cosmology
   bounces; Penrose's CCC has no crunch). Known problem: entropy accumulates from cycle to cycle (Tolman), so a fresh
   start with "maximum" dark energy needs an entropy reset. The snap point is "unquantifiable" as stated: fine as
   untestable, but the path toward it is testable.
 - TESTABLE NOW: the shape and timing of the dark-energy density history against the chosen branching proxy; a
   downward trend in dark-energy density heading toward zero. UNTESTABLE: the snap, the 0D collapse, the rebirth,
   other timelines.

ENTRY 5 · 2026-10-31 · Gregory Mandarano (adopts the baseline form from the Entry 3 critique)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DEFINITION: Dark energy is a measure of how many quantum interactions are taking place in the universe at a given
cosmic time, RELATIVE TO A BASELINE. Negative dark energy does not mean negative interaction; it means fewer
interactions than are required to maintain a stable timeline. Space keeps expanding, so the amount of quantum
interaction falls over time (even if stellar mass keeps rising), and dark energy falls with it.
  Working form: ρ_DE(t) ∝ [ I(t) − I_0 ],  I = cosmic quantum-interaction rate, I_0 = the stability baseline.
NOTES (planning chat):
 - The definition of I is now the whole theory. RAW interaction rate fails: the early universe was an opaque plasma
   in which photons scattered constantly; at recombination (~380,000 years) the universe became transparent and
   the photon scattering rate collapsed by many orders of magnitude. A raw rate would make dark energy enormous
   early (ruled out by the CMB) and would not rise toward cosmic noon.
 - Expansion dilutes interactions in intergalactic gas and radiation, but not inside galaxies, which do not expand;
   stars there keep interacting. The interactions that RISE to cosmic noon and fall since are star-driven,
   irreversible ones (entropy production by starlight and dust; cf. Bousso et al. 2007, Entry 3). So I is most
   naturally an irreversible / entropy-producing interaction rate, not a raw count. To be derived, not chosen to fit.
 - Per what volume: I must be defined per comoving volume, per physical volume, or for the whole observable
   universe. The choice changes the shape and must be locked.
 - Knobs: the proportionality constant and I_0 are two numbers. Today's measured dark energy fixes one combination;
   the other is free unless derived. Plus the choice of I.
 - Testable form: the ratio of today's dark energy to its peak value, and the peak date, follow from I(t) alone
   once I_0 is set. A future crossing below zero follows if I(t) keeps falling below I_0.

ENTRY 6 · 2026-10-31 · Gregory Mandarano (planner's extension marked)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IDEA: The interactions that count are the ones that MATTER: those with consequences. A photon striking a rock that
never interacts with anything again, because space carries it away, is meaningless and does not count.
NOTES (planning chat):
 - Nearest established physics (Tier 1–2): in decoherence theory, a branch becomes effectively real when its record
   is copied redundantly into the environment (Zurek's "quantum Darwinism"). "Interactions that matter" ≈
   interactions whose records spread. Bousso et al. 2007 count only entropy produced inside a CAUSAL DIAMOND, the
   region whose events can still reach an observer: the same instinct, already in the literature.
 - PLANNER'S EXTENSION (not Gregory's): expansion cuts consequence chains at the cosmic event horizon. Accelerated
   expansion pushes more matter beyond reach, so fewer interactions have consequences, so dark energy falls. This
   is a feedback loop: dark energy suppresses the very interactions that sustain it. It gives a physical reason for
   the decline to begin once acceleration takes hold (~5–6 billion years ago) and could move the predicted peak later
   than cosmic noon. CAUTION: the planning chat already knows current fits peak near z ≈ 0.4–0.5 (design data, not
   fenced). Any peak date must come from the derivation, and the test is on fenced data (F3, F4).
 - Definition problem: "has consequences" depends on the FUTURE, which would make today's dark energy depend on what
   happens later (and on the future expansion it causes). Must be recast as something computable from the present
   state, e.g. "lies within the future causal reach of other matter", or "record copied into at least N systems".
 - Most interactions DO have downstream effects (the rock warms and re-radiates). The measure therefore needs a
   weighting (how far consequences spread) with a cutoff; the horizon is the natural cutoff.

ENTRY 7 · 2026-10-31 · Gregory Mandarano
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IDEA: A timeline with no real consequences for its future has no reason to continue existing. The everpresent now
(v1's moving present) is a BOW WAVE, not a single point, and the strength of the bow wave IS dark energy. One version
of the model: when dark energy reaches zero the bow wave stops moving forward; when dark energy goes negative the bow
wave moves BACKWARD at an accelerating rate, time runs in reverse, and the universe collapses. The rate of time is a
reflection of two things: velocity and the amount of dark energy in the universe.
NOTES (planning chat):
 - "No purpose to continue" restated in physical terms: a timeline with no reachable consequences is no longer
   sustained (consistent with Entries 5–6).
 - Relativity already makes clock rates depend on velocity (special relativity) and gravity (general relativity).
   This adds a cosmic term. A change in the rate of time that affects EVERY clock equally cannot be measured; to be
   physics it must change a measurable ratio.
 - Existing measurement: distant supernovae (DES 2024, out to z ~ 1) and quasars evolve slower by the factor (1+z)
   predicted by standard expansion. A dark-energy-dependent term in the rate of time would show up as a deviation
   from (1+z) that follows dark energy's history. Current data constrain it to be small. A candidate test.
 - Negative dark energy → time reversal: separate from a crunch (in general relativity a collapse runs forward in
   time). Precedent (historical, Tier 2): Thomas Gold proposed that the arrow of time reverses when the universe
   contracts; Stephen Hawking argued for it in 1985 and later withdrew it after work by Don Page and his student
   Raymond Laflamme. The snap, stop and reversal are future events: UNTESTABLE bin.

ENTRY 10 · 2026-10-31 · Gregory Mandarano (approach), Gemini (equations, relayed), planner critique · NO FITS YET
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
APPROACH (Gregory, after reviewing the Entry 9 candidates; formalized by Gemini): dark energy is a STOCK of bundle
tension. Causally productive macroscopic interactions (decoherence events whose consequences can still propagate)
pump tension in; the bundle's natural unwinding (v1's γ_u) bleeds it out:
    dρ_de/dt + 3H(γ_u/E)ρ_de = Q(a)   ⇔   w_eff = −1 + γ_u/E − Q/(3Hρ_de)     (algebra verified)
The stock rises (w_eff < −1) while pumping beats unwinding, peaks when they balance (w_eff = −1), and weakens after.
Acceleration shrinks the causally connected region, so fewer interactions count: a self-limiting feedback.
Gemini's timeline numbers check out for ΛCDM-like values (q = 0 at z ≈ 0.65; matter–DE equality z ≈ 0.3) and for
v1's CPL fits (w = −1 at z ≈ 0.39–0.40). Gemini cites holographic dark energy (Li 2004) as a cousin.
PLANNER CRITIQUE (Claude Opus 5.5):
 1. Stock vs rate: an accumulating stock CAN rise while star formation falls, which removes Entry 9's amplitude
    objection to rate-type sources (that objection applied to Entry 5's instantaneous form).
 2. Wrong horizon: the comoving EVENT horizon shrinks at all times, not only after acceleration begins. The
    quantity that switches at acceleration onset is the comoving HUBBLE radius 1/(aH), which grows while the
    expansion decelerates and shrinks once it accelerates. The Hubble radius is also local in time, which avoids
    the future-dependence problem (Entry 6); the event horizon is the true "can ever be reached" boundary but
    depends on the whole future. A design choice for Gregory.
 3. Energy budget: all starlight ever emitted is ~4e-6 of the critical density (~6e-6 of dark energy). Q cannot be
    paid for by baryonic processes. In general relativity the energy must come from somewhere: the natural source is
    the dark sector itself (dark matter ↔ dark energy exchange, i.e. Entry 1's frays). This is an interacting
    dark-energy model (Tier 2 literature) and predicts that dark matter does not dilute exactly as a^-3: testable
    in structure growth (fenced F1, KiDS lensing).
 4. Holographic dark energy runs the OPPOSITE way: its w = −1/3 − (2/3)√Ω_de/c falls over time (w_a > 0 in CPL
    terms), whereas the DESI hint has w rising toward today (w_a < 0). A cousin in mechanism, not in result.
 5. Correction to Entry 9: at z ≈ 2.3 dark energy is only ~6% of the total energy, so data barely constrain
    ρ_de at cosmic noon; the "16–20% below today at z = 2" figure is mostly the CPL shape extrapolating. The
    constrained window is roughly z ≲ 1.5.
 6. Knobs: γ_u, the pumping normalization, the source history Q(a) choice, and (for a crunch) a cohesion baseline.
    Two free rates can place the peak anywhere, so matching z ≈ 0.4 by itself is fitting. Predictions must come
    from what the knobs do not control: the shape of w(z) over z ≈ 0.5–1.5 given a fixed published source history,
    and the dark-matter dilution signature.
 7. Crunch: with Q ≥ 0 and the unwinding term, ρ_de only decays toward zero. A crunch needs Entry 5's baseline
    (a constant negative "cohesion" term, i.e. a negative cosmological constant under a positive decaying tension).

     is N − 1 = a₀/g_tot. This gives the simple-ν radial acceleration relation and the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation.
     K cancels from every observable.
   - Fading is desync of the cross-gap coupling. A faded branch stops pulling on us and its matter stays in its own
     branch; nothing is deposited into our ρ_dm. (If it were, every galaxy would create dark matter without limit,
     violating T-b and V4.) "Two life stages" is story imagery only; the cloud is a separate, fixed stock.
   - The pulse (Gregory): the birth–fade cycle at the bow wave is the mechanism itself. Births form a Poisson train
     with mean a₀/g_tot and fluctuations falling as N^(−1/2), which average to the smooth relation.
   - A stationary interference pattern between strings is EXPLORATORY only. It requires copies to add as amplitudes,
     which the model does not yet license, and it adds nothing to the galaxy law.

3. GRAVITY RULE (THE MODEL; chosen by the planner on Gregory's written authorisation, thread files 053 and 060):
   ∇·[μ(|∇Φ|/a₀)∇Φ] = 4πG(ρ_b + ρ_dm), μ(x) = x/(1 + x), from the Bekenstein & Milgrom 1984 action (ApJ 286, 7), so
   momentum, angular momentum and energy are conserved. The boost acts on all matter a branch carries, ordinary
   matter and cloud alike. The baryons-only alternative was rejected: it has no action, lets offset systems
   accelerate themselves, and could be repaired only by letting ordinary and dark matter fall differently. QUMOND
   may be used only as a computing shortcut where it coincides with AQUAL (spherical symmetry) or with the difference
   checked. Light bending follows the same potential as the motion of matter (Φ = Ψ), and gravitational waves travel
   at c; both are stated rules, with AeST (Skordis & Złośnik 2021) as the published relativistic theory showing they
   can hold together.

4. BACKGROUND-DECELERATION RULE (THE MODEL, fixed before any test): g_tot includes the universe's own deceleration
   across the system's size, |ä/a| L. For a bound galaxy or cluster, L = r measured from its centre; for a linear
   perturbation of comoving wavenumber k, L = a/k and g_tot = (g_bg² + g_pec²)^(1/2) (thread file 044 §2.1). At
   recombination this is 4.4–6.0 × 10⁴ a₀ on 100–135 kpc scales (the sound horizon), so the boost there is about
   2 × 10⁻⁵. For 2–5 kpc scales the boost switches on at 1 + z ≈ 100–134. At late times the rule is
   negligible on galaxy and cluster scales (it falls below a₀ for every scale under about 1.4–1.6 Gpc at z = 0–1).

7. THE CLOUD:
   - Shape: cored (Plummer form), one universal core radius R_core for all systems.
   - R_core: no derivation exists. It is one honest dial (Option A), set on Bucket 1 design data after the video's
     release and before the fence opens. The sound-horizon ansatz (Option B) was dropped under R5.
   - Cloud mass per system (Gregory, thread file 063; options in 061, checked in 062): every system carries the
     cosmic share, M_cloud = (Ω_dm/Ω_b) M_b ≈ 5.36 M_b, with no dial. This is an explicit model assumption, not an
     identity. Rejected: a second fitted dial (weaker test; too little cloud for cluster lensing if fitted low), one
     central density for all clouds (swamps dwarfs, starves clusters), clouds only around groups and clusters (an
     unmotivated threshold), and a share set by retained gas (hidden dials; worsens the outer-curve risk). Because
     the cloud's share inside radius r depends only on r/R_core, dwarfs measured inside ~10 kpc are unaffected.
   - Consequences, recorded before any data is examined: because the cloud's own field is boosted (item 3), outer
     rotation curves feel it (for M_b = 5 × 10¹⁰ M☉ and R_core = 110 kpc, extra pull of about 5 / 11 / 19% at
     30 / 40 / 50 kpc; about 1 / 2 / 4% for R_core = 200 kpc); the radial acceleration and Tully–Fisher relations hold
     unchanged only where the enclosed cloud is small compared with M_b; at weak-lensing radii the pull rises to
     √(1 + 5.36) ≈ 2.5 times the ordinary-matter value, a step rather than a bump.

9. ROPE STRUCTURE (interpretation only; no term in any equation):
   - Strings exchange between neighbouring strands inside a rope, timed by the twist: a string can cross only while
     it faces a neighbouring strand. Strings on the rope's outer surface face the Void and cannot cross. A string that
     leaves one strand lands in another, so the rope keeps its total.
   - Every string carries frays. Inside a rope, frays lie between strings and strands; taut frays hold dark energy's
     tension (ρ_T) and slack frays are the free frays (ρ_dm). On the rope's outer surface the outward frays have
     nothing to hook onto and are slack.
   - Between ropes there is only the outer strings' fuzz, which never reaches the next rope. Nothing crosses the Void,
     consistent with no-signalling between branches (Polchinski 1991).
   - The gap between ropes is decoherence that cannot be undone, not a force: no field, no potential, no dial. Ropes
     touch only at the 0D point (Entry 39 §5).
   - Dark energy weakening is the twist releasing against drag. The reading "net string drift = γ_u" is withdrawn.
